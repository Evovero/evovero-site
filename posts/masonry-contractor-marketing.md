<p>Masonry work rarely gets found by accident. A homeowner with a leaning retaining wall or a chimney shedding brick searches for help the same day they notice the problem, and they hire whoever answers first with proof they know what they're doing. If your <a href="https://www.evovero.com/industries/masonry/">masonry marketing</a> isn't built around that moment, you're losing jobs to a competitor with a worse crew and a better online presence.</p>

<h2>Start with your Google Business Profile, not your website</h2>
<p>For most masonry contractors, the Google Business Profile does more work than the website. It's what shows up in the map pack when someone searches "tuckpointing near me" or "chimney repair [city]," and it's increasingly what feeds Google's AI-generated local answers too. A profile that's thin loses to a profile that's complete, even when the thin one belongs to the better contractor.</p>
<p>A few things matter more than people think. Every field should be filled in, not just the obvious ones. Your primary category needs to match what homeowners actually search, not just what sounds most accurate to you (search a few of your target terms in an incognito map search and see what category the top results use). Photos should go up regularly, not just once during setup, because profiles that show ongoing activity tend to hold their visibility better than ones that go quiet.</p>
<p>One thing to watch closely: Google has been cracking down on business names that get stuffed with extra keywords to try to rank higher ("Smith Masonry Chimney Repair Tuckpointing"). That's a fast way to get a profile suspended. Keep your listed name exactly what's on your door and your paperwork.</p>

<h2>Reviews are a ranking signal and a trust signal at the same time</h2>
<p>Review volume and recency both factor into how Google ranks a local listing, and they're the first thing a homeowner reads before they call. A masonry contractor with 40 recent reviews beats one with 200 reviews that stopped coming in two years ago. Ask for a review at the moment a job wraps, when the homeowner is standing in front of finished work, not in a follow-up text three weeks later that gets ignored.</p>
<p>Respond to every review, good and bad. A short, professional reply to a negative review often does more for a prospect reading it than the review itself did damage. Silence reads as either not caring or not being around. This is the kind of thing that's easy to let slide when you're running jobs all day, which is why <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/reputation/">reputation management</a> is worth treating as its own task rather than an afterthought.</p>

<h2>Local SEO and AI visibility are becoming the same problem</h2>
<p>The way people search is shifting. A growing share of local searches now surface an AI-generated summary before they show a list of websites, and that summary pulls from the same signals that drive traditional local rankings: your Google Business Profile, your reviews, and consistent business information across directories (your name, address, and phone number matching everywhere they appear). If those are solid, you're positioned for both the old kind of search and the new kind. If they're inconsistent, an AI summary is just as likely to skip you as a human searcher is.</p>
<p>Your website still matters here. Service pages that actually name what you do (tuckpointing, chimney rebuilds, retaining wall repair, historic restoration) in plain language, tied to the towns you serve, give both Google and AI tools something specific to match a search against. A single vague "masonry services" page does neither job well.</p>

<h2>Paid ads work, but only once the free stuff is solid</h2>
<p><a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/google-ads/">Google Ads</a> and <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/meta-ads/">Meta ads</a> can put a masonry contractor in front of a homeowner the moment they're searching or scrolling, and for a trade with real urgency (a crumbling chimney, a wall that's about to fail) that speed matters. But ads sending traffic to a weak Google Business Profile or a bare-bones website waste money. Get the foundation in place first: complete profile, real reviews, service pages that speak to what you actually do. Ads amplify what's already working. They don't fix what isn't.</p>

<h2>Make it easy for a homeowner to say yes</h2>
<p>All the visibility in the world doesn't help if the person who finds you can't quickly tell you're the right call. A homeowner comparing three masonry contractors on their phone isn't reading paragraphs. They're scanning for photos of real work, a phone number they can tap without hunting, and something that tells them you do the specific job they need (chimney rebuild, not just "masonry services").</p>
<p>This is where a lot of otherwise solid marketing falls apart. Traffic comes in from search or ads, lands on a slow or cluttered site, and leaves without calling. A <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/web-design/">website built to convert</a> doesn't need to be flashy. It needs a clear phone number above the fold, real project photos instead of stock images, and service pages that name the actual work rather than vague category language. Fixing this is often cheaper than the ad spend it takes to compensate for it.</p>

<h2>Own your marketing instead of renting your leads</h2>
<p>A lot of masonry contractors start with a lead-generation platform because it's the fastest way to get the phone ringing. The tradeoff shows up later: you're paying per lead indefinitely, competing with other contractors for the same homeowner, and building someone else's brand instead of your own. A website, a Google Business Profile, and an ad account that you own keep working for you long after any individual campaign ends, and every review and every published page adds to something that's actually yours.</p>
<p>That's the shift worth making once you have steady work: move spend away from rented leads and toward a presence that compounds. It's slower in month one. It's cheaper and more durable in year two.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What's the single highest-leverage thing a masonry contractor can do for marketing right now?</h3>
<p>Get the Google Business Profile fully filled out, matched to a category homeowners actually search, and updated with new photos regularly. It's the first thing most homeowners see and it feeds both traditional search and newer AI-generated local answers.</p>

<h3>How many reviews does a masonry contractor need to compete?</h3>
<p>There's no fixed number that guarantees ranking, but recency matters as much as total volume. A steady trickle of new reviews from recent jobs outperforms a large stockpile that stopped growing.</p>

<h3>Should a masonry contractor run ads before fixing their Google Business Profile?</h3>
<p>No. Ads drive traffic to whatever's already there. If the profile and website are weak, paid traffic converts poorly and the spend is largely wasted. Fix the foundation first.</p>

<h3>Does AI search change how masonry contractors should think about SEO?</h3>
<p>It changes where the summary shows up, not what feeds it. Complete, accurate, consistent business information and real reviews are what both traditional local rankings and AI-generated answers pull from.</p>

<h3>Is a website still necessary if most traffic comes through Google Business Profile?</h3>
<p>Yes. The website gives Google and AI tools something specific to match a search against, especially when service pages name exact services and the towns you serve rather than staying generic.</p>
