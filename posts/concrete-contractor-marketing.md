<p>If you pour concrete for a living and you want more driveway and patio work, the fastest path is almost never a new logo or a bigger ad budget. It is making sure you show up when someone in your service area searches for a concrete contractor, and then answering them before your competitor does. Those two things decide most of it.</p>

<p>I work with concrete contractors, and the pattern repeats. The owner is good at the work, busy enough to be tired, and losing jobs at two specific points: he is invisible in the map results, and he cannot get to the phone while he is on a pour. Here is how I think about fixing both.</p>

<h2>Start With Google Business Profile, Not Your Website</h2>

<p>When a homeowner searches "concrete driveway contractor near me," the map results sit above the regular listings. That block is fed by your Google Business Profile, and for most concrete contractors it is the single highest leverage asset you own. It is also free.</p>

<h3>Set It Up as a Service Area Business</h3>

<p>Most concrete contractors work out of a shop or a home, not a storefront where customers show up. Google supports that. You verify with a real address behind the scenes, then hide it from public view and list the cities you actually serve instead. Hiding the address is the correct setup for a business that travels to the customer, not a workaround.</p>

<p>Be realistic about the service area. Listing every town within two hours does not extend your reach, and it dilutes the relevance signals for the towns you actually want. Name the cities you would genuinely drive to for a driveway job and stop there.</p>

<h3>Get Your Categories Right</h3>

<p>Your primary category does more work than almost anything else on the profile. "Concrete contractor" is not the same as "paving contractor" or "masonry contractor," and picking the wrong primary quietly keeps you out of results you should be winning. Add secondary categories for the other work you really do, but choose the primary based on the jobs you most want to book.</p>

<h3>Keep Reviews Coming In Steadily</h3>

<p>A steady trickle of reviews consistently does more for local visibility than a burst of them followed by a year of silence. Steady also reads as more credible to the person deciding whether to call you. The practical version: ask at the moment the customer is happiest, which is when they are standing on the finished slab, and make it one tap by texting them the link.</p>

<h3>Post Photos of Actual Jobs</h3>

<p>Concrete is a visual purchase. Homeowners want to see a stamped patio that looks like the one they are imagining, in a neighborhood that looks like theirs. Before and after shots from real jobs beat anything from a stock library, and they give you something to publish regularly without writing a word.</p>

<h2>Your Website Has One Job</h2>

<p>The website is not a brochure. It exists to convert someone who already found you into a phone call or a form submission. For a concrete contractor, that means a few specific things.</p>

<p>You need a separate page for each service you want to be found for, driveways, patios, slabs, stamped and decorative work, rather than one page that lists everything. You need pages for the towns you serve, with real content about working in those towns instead of the same paragraph with the city name swapped out. And you need your phone number visible without scrolling, tappable on a phone, on every single page.</p>

<p>One more thing that matters now more than it used to: AI assistants and search summaries pull from the same signals as local search, so the answers they give about "who does stamped concrete in my area" lean on your profile, your reviews, and whether your site clearly states what you do and where you do it. Vague site copy costs you in more places than it used to. I go deeper on this in <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/seo/">how I approach local SEO</a>.</p>

<h2>Speed to Lead Is Where Most Concrete Jobs Are Lost</h2>

<p>Here is the uncomfortable part. A homeowner planning a driveway replacement is not calling one contractor. They are calling three, and in a lot of cases they hire whoever calls back first and shows up to measure.</p>

<p>You are on a job site with your hands in wet concrete. You physically cannot answer. That is not a discipline problem, it is a structural one, and the fix is structural too.</p>

<h3>Cover the Calls You Cannot Take</h3>

<p>Missed call text back is the cheapest version: the moment you miss a call, the caller automatically gets a text saying you are on a job and asking what they need. That alone recovers a meaningful share of calls that would otherwise go to the next name on the list.</p>

<p>The fuller version is an <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/ai-automation/">AI receptionist that answers every call</a>, asks what kind of work they need, gets the address and the timeline, and drops a qualified lead in your phone. You call back with the details already in hand instead of starting cold.</p>

<h3>Respond to Form Fills Immediately</h3>

<p>The same logic applies to your contact form. A quote request that sits until evening is a quote request your competitor already answered. Automatic acknowledgment plus a real reply the same day changes close rates more than most advertising does.</p>

<h2>When Paid Ads Make Sense</h2>

<p>Paid traffic works for concrete, but it works best after the basics above are in place. Sending paid clicks to a weak site and an unanswered phone just makes the leak more expensive.</p>

<h3>Google Ads Catches Existing Demand</h3>

<p>Someone typing "concrete patio contractor" plus a town name is ready to buy. That is the highest intent traffic available to you, and it is worth paying for. The mistakes I see most often are running one broad campaign for every service instead of separating driveways from patios, ignoring negative keywords so budget bleeds into people searching for jobs or DIY instructions, and pointing every ad at the homepage instead of the matching service page. My take on <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/google-ads/">running Google Ads for service businesses</a> covers the setup.</p>

<p>Local Services Ads are worth checking as well, though availability varies by trade and by market and the category list keeps changing. Check what is available for your ZIP code before you plan around them.</p>

<h3>Meta Ads Create Demand That Was Not There Yet</h3>

<p>Facebook and Instagram work differently. Nobody scrolling is searching for a driveway. But a homeowner who sees a genuinely good before and after from a house two neighborhoods over starts thinking about their own cracked driveway. That is <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/meta-ads/">what Meta ads do well for contractors</a>: they generate interest in the off season and fill the pipeline for spring.</p>

<h2>Put It in Order</h2>

<p>If you only do a few things this month, do them in this sequence. Claim and fully build out your Google Business Profile with correct categories and a realistic service area. Set up a way to capture calls you miss. Get your service pages and town pages saying something specific. Then, and only then, turn on paid traffic. Skipping to the last step is how contractors conclude that marketing does not work for concrete.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Do concrete contractors really need a website if they have a Google profile?</h3>

<p>Yes. Google reads your website to decide how much to trust your profile, and homeowners comparing three contractors will look. A profile with no substantial site behind it caps how far you can climb in local results and gives serious buyers nothing to evaluate.</p>

<h3>How long does local SEO take for a concrete contractor?</h3>

<p>Profile improvements can shift things within weeks. Ranking gains from new service and location pages usually take a few months to compound, because Google needs to see the content, the reviews, and the consistency hold up over time. It builds rather than switches on.</p>

<h3>Should I advertise year round in a seasonal climate?</h3>

<p>Search ads make most sense when people are actually searching, so they follow your season. Social ads are the opposite. Running them in the slow months builds a list of interested homeowners you can book as soon as the weather turns, instead of starting from zero every spring.</p>

<h3>What is the single biggest mistake concrete contractors make?</h3>

<p>Not responding fast enough. Ranking well and then taking two days to return a call hands the job to someone else. Fixing response time usually produces more revenue than any change to advertising, and it costs less.</p>
