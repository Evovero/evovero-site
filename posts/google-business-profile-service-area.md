<p>If you drive to your customers instead of having them come to you, set your Google Business Profile up as a service area business: hide your street address and list the cities or postal codes you cover, up to 20 of them. Google wants the area to stay within roughly two hours of driving from where you are based. Done right, this tells Google where to show you. Done wrong, it can get your profile suspended.</p>

<p>I see owners get this wrong constantly, usually in one of two directions. They leave a home address showing for the whole world, or they list every town in the state hoping to show up everywhere. Neither helps. Here is how I set it up.</p>

<h2>Do You Hide Your Address or Show It?</h2>

<p>The deciding question is simple: do customers come to your address? Google's own guidance on <a href="https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481">service areas on a Business Profile</a> says that if you do not serve customers at your business address, you should remove the address from your profile.</p>

<p>For most of the trades I work with, that settles it. A concrete crew, a tree service, a mobile detailer, or a cleaning company does the work at the customer's property. Nobody visits your shop or your garage, so the address comes off.</p>

<p>The exception is a hybrid business. If customers do walk in, say you run a showroom or a yard where people pick up material, and you also travel to jobs, the same Google page says you can keep the address showing and add a service area on top.</p>

<h3>Why this matters for a home-based owner</h3>

<p>Plenty of owners run the business from a house. If that address stays public, strangers see where you live and customers may drive there expecting a storefront. Hiding it protects your privacy and sets the right expectation. Your profile then shows the area you serve instead of a pin on your street.</p>

<h2>How to Hide Your Address and Set the Area</h2>

<p>Google's <a href="https://support.google.com/business/answer/10514137">Business Profile setup guide</a> lays out the steps. In Google Search, open Edit profile, then Location. In Google Maps, open Edit profile, then Business information, then Location. Click the edit icon next to the business location and turn off the option that shows your address to customers.</p>

<p>Once the address is hidden, the profile displays only your service area. If you never entered one, Google picks a default local area for you, and you should not leave it that way. Edit it so it matches where your crew actually works.</p>

<h2>Choosing Which Areas to List</h2>

<p>You can set up to 20 service areas, built from cities, postal codes, or other regions. That is a ceiling, not a target. I treat the list as a statement of where I would honestly send a truck on a normal weekday.</p>

<h3>Stay inside a realistic drive</h3>

<p>Google recommends keeping your overall area within about two hours of driving from your business location, though it acknowledges some businesses can justify more. For most local trades the honest answer is much tighter. If a job forty-five minutes out is not worth your fuel and time, do not list that town.</p>

<h3>List where you want jobs, not everywhere</h3>

<p>A short list of towns you actively want beats a long list you will never answer calls from. Start with your home base and the neighboring towns you already serve, then add the next ring only if you are ready to take work there. Then back it up on your website with a real page for each major town, which is the part that helps you rank for searches that include a place name. That is the foundation I build in my <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/seo/">local SEO work</a>.</p>

<h2>The Mistakes That Cost Owners Rankings</h2>

<h3>Listing a fake or virtual office</h3>

<p>Do not put a rented mailbox or a friend's storefront on your profile to look more established. It misrepresents your business to customers and to Google. If you do not have a real location customers visit, hide the address and say so honestly.</p>

<h3>Making a second profile for every town</h3>

<p>It is tempting to create one profile per suburb to look bigger. Google's setup guide says to create only one Business Profile per metropolitan area, and that multiple profiles for the same business in one area can lead to account suspension. One accurate profile with a good service area is stronger than five thin ones.</p>

<h3>Forgetting to update when things change</h3>

<p>Crews move, you take on a new territory, or you decide a far town is not worth the drive. Review the list a couple of times a year. An outdated area sends you calls you do not want and hides you from the ones you do.</p>

<h3>Ignoring the rest of the profile</h3>

<p>A perfect service area does not carry a weak profile. Your categories, services, photos, and reviews still do most of the ranking work. <h2>Check Your Work</h2>

<p>After you save, search for your main service plus a town you listed, on your phone, from outside your own house. Look at what shows. Your profile should display a service area rather than a street address, the name and phone number should match your website exactly, and the hours should be right. Then ask a friend in one of the outer towns to search the same thing. If you never appear for the places you listed, the problem is usually the rest of the profile, not the area setting.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Should I hide my address on Google Business Profile?</h3>

<p>Hide it if you do not serve customers at your address, which is true for most mobile trades. Google's guidance says to remove the address in that case. If customers do visit your location and you also travel to them, you can keep it showing and add a service area.</p>

<h3>How many service areas can I add?</h3>

<p>Google allows up to 20 service areas, based on cities, postal codes, or other regions. That is a limit, not a goal. I recommend listing only the places you genuinely want jobs and are willing to drive to, then building website pages for the most important ones.</p>

<h3>How far can my service area extend?</h3>

<p>Google recommends staying within about two hours of driving from your business location, though some businesses can justify a larger area. In practice, most local trades should be tighter than that. List the towns where a job is actually worth the drive and your calls will improve.</p>

<h3>Can I have a separate profile for each town I serve?</h3>

<p>No. Google's setup guide says to create one Business Profile per metropolitan area, and that multiple profiles for the same business in one area can lead to suspension. Use one profile with an accurate service area, and use your website to give each town its own page.</p>

<h3>Will hiding my address hurt my ranking?</h3>

<p>Hiding the address is the correct setup for a service area business, so it should not be treated as a penalty. Proximity still matters in local search, so you may rank best near your base. Strong reviews, complete services, and consistent business details help you rank across the rest of your area.</p>
