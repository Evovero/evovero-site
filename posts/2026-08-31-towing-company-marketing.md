<p>Towing is the least forgiving local marketing category I work in, and the reason is simple: the customer is standing on a shoulder with a dead phone battery and they are calling the first number that answers. Not the best company. The first one that shows up and picks up. Everything below is built around that one fact.</p>

<p>Here is how I think about getting a towing company found, and in what order I would spend the effort.</p>

<h2>Speed to answer beats everything else you could do</h2>

<p>Most marketing advice starts with visibility. For towing I start with the phone, because visibility you cannot convert is just a bigger bill.</p>

<p>A stranded driver typically opens Google Maps, taps the top result, and calls. If nobody answers, they tap the next one. They are not leaving a voicemail and they are not filling out a form. That means a missed call is not a delayed lead, it is a lost job, and it went straight to a competitor who did nothing smarter than answer.</p>

<p>Before I touch a single ad, I want to know what happens to a call at 2am, during a storm when three come in at once, and when the operator is under a truck with his hands full. Usually the honest answer is that some of them ring out. That is the first thing to fix, and it is worth more than any ranking improvement, because it multiplies everything downstream.</p>

<p>A 24 hour answering service works. So does a well built <a href="/services/ai-automation/">AI receptionist</a> that picks up on the first ring, captures the location and vehicle, and texts the operator. What does not work is hoping. If you fix nothing else this year, fix the missed call.</p>

<h2>Your Google Business Profile is the storefront, not your website</h2>

<p>For emergency search, the map pack is the storefront. Most of the calls come from there, not from a website visit.</p>

<p>Google ranks local results on three things: proximity, relevance and prominence. For a towing company, which is usually a service area business rather than a walk-in shop, proximity carries less weight than it does for a restaurant, because there is no address customers are traveling to. That shifts the burden onto relevance and prominence, which are the two things you can actually influence.</p>

<p>Practically, that means:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Hide the address if you do not serve customers at your location.</strong> Google's own guidance for service area businesses is to hide it, and doing it their way avoids a suspension risk that can take your listing offline at the worst possible time.</li>
<li><strong>List service areas granularly.</strong> Naming the actual towns and corridors you cover, rather than drawing one enormous circle, gives Google something specific to match against a specific query.</li>
<li><strong>Get the primary category right and use secondary categories deliberately.</strong> Towing, roadside assistance and vehicle recovery are not the same category and they do not surface for the same searches.</li>
<li><strong>Put real photos on it.</strong> Your trucks, your operators, actual jobs. Stock photos of somebody else's wrecker tell a customer nothing and tell Google less.</li>
<li><strong>Answer the questions section and post occasionally.</strong> Both are prominence signals and almost nobody in this trade does either.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Reviews are the tiebreaker, and towing makes them hard</h2>

<p>When three companies appear in the map pack and all three are equally close, review count and rating decide who gets tapped. That is the whole competition, resolved in about two seconds.</p>

<p>Towing has a structural problem here: nobody is in a good mood. The customer just had the worst part of their day. Asking for a review at the scene rarely lands.</p>

<p>What works better is asking later, by text, once the situation has resolved and they are grateful rather than stressed. A short message a day or two after the job, with a direct link, converts far better than a business card handed over in the rain. Building that into your process rather than leaving it to memory is what separates a company with forty reviews from one with four hundred.</p>

<p>Respond to the bad ones plainly and without arguing. A calm, specific reply to a one star review persuades the next reader more than the review itself damages you.</p>

<h2>Paid search: where towing is different</h2>

<p>Google's Local Services Ads opened the towing category nationwide in October 2024, and that changed the top of the results page for this trade. Local Services Ads sit above the standard search ads and above the map pack, you are verified through Google's screening process, and you are charged per lead rather than per click.</p>

<p>For emergency work that structure fits the job better than a traditional click auction does, because a click from someone comparison shopping at their desk is worth very little to a tow operator and a phone call from someone on a shoulder is worth a great deal.</p>

<p>Standard <a href="/services/google-ads/">Google Ads</a> still has a place, particularly for the searches Local Services Ads does not cover well and for scheduled work like transport, dealer moves and equipment hauling. The mistake I see most often is running one campaign for both emergency and scheduled work. They have different urgency, different keywords, different ad copy and different hours. Splitting them is usually the single biggest improvement available in a towing account.</p>

<h2>The website's job is narrower than you think</h2>

<p>A towing site is not there to impress anyone. It is there to do three things: make the phone number impossible to miss, prove you are real, and rank for the searches that are not emergencies.</p>

<p>That last one is where the money quietly is. Emergency searches are competitive and expensive. Searches like flatbed transport for a lowered car, junk vehicle removal, equipment hauling, dealer transfers and accident storage questions are lower volume, much cheaper to win, and often better jobs. Service pages and honest local content built around those terms compound over time in a way paid traffic never does. That is what <a href="/services/seo/">local SEO work</a> is actually buying you.</p>

<p>Two practical things on the site itself. Put the phone number in the header as a tap to call link on every page, because a person on a phone should never have to hunt. And build a page for each service area town, with real detail about the roads and conditions there, not a template with the town name swapped in. Google is good at recognizing the second one.</p>

<h2>Where I would start</h2>

<p>If you run a towing company and you can only do a few things this quarter, in order:</p>

<ol>
<li>Make sure every call gets answered, every time.</li>
<li>Fix and fill out the Google Business Profile properly, categories, service areas, photos.</li>
<li>Build a review request into the job close so it happens without anyone remembering.</li>
<li>Get on Local Services Ads if your market supports it.</li>
<li>Then, and only then, worry about the website and the content.</li>
</ol>

<p>That order is deliberate. Every item pays for itself before the next one starts, and doing them out of order is how operators end up paying for traffic that rings out.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>What is the fastest way to get more towing calls?</h3>
<p>Answer the phone every time. Most towing companies lose more jobs to unanswered calls than to poor rankings, because a stranded driver simply calls the next listing. Fixing call coverage costs less than advertising and improves the return on every marketing dollar you were already spending.</p>

<h3>Should a towing company use Local Services Ads or regular Google Ads?</h3>
<p>Both have a role. Local Services Ads opened to towing nationwide in October 2024, sit above search ads and the map pack, and charge per lead, which suits emergency work. Regular Google Ads is better for scheduled work like transport, dealer moves and equipment hauling.</p>

<h3>Do I need a physical address for my towing business on Google?</h3>
<p>Not if customers do not come to you. Google treats towing companies as service area businesses and advises hiding the address in that case. Hiding it and defining your service areas by town is the compliant approach and reduces the risk of a listing suspension.</p>

<h3>How do I get reviews when customers are having a bad day?</h3>
<p>Ask later, not at the scene. A short text with a direct review link a day or two after the job catches people once the stress has passed and gratitude has set in. Building that step into your job close, rather than relying on memory, is what produces consistent volume.</p>

<h3>Is a website even worth it for a towing company?</h3>
<p>Yes, but for a different reason than most owners assume. Emergency calls mostly come from the map pack. The website earns its keep by ranking for the non emergency work, transport, junk removal, equipment hauling and dealer moves, which is less competitive and often more profitable.</p>
