<p>Facebook and Instagram ads work for local service businesses, but not the way Google Ads works. Google catches people who are already searching for you. Meta puts you in front of people who were not looking, which means the ad has to create the want, and the follow-up has to close the gap. When owners tell me Meta ads did not work, the problem is almost always one of those two things, not the platform.</p>

<p>Here is how I think about running them, and what has actually changed on the platform recently that affects how you should set them up.</p>

<h2>Meta Creates Demand, Google Captures It</h2>

<p>Somebody typing "driveway replacement near me" into Google has already decided they have a problem. Your job is to be there and be credible. That is why <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/google-ads/">Google Ads for service businesses</a> tend to produce leads that convert quickly.</p>

<p>Nobody opens Instagram looking for a mason. They see a stamped patio, a repaired chimney, a cleaned-up yard, and they think about the thing they have been putting off for two years. That lead is real, but it is earlier. They have not gotten other bids. They may not have a budget yet. Some of them are six months out.</p>

<p>This matters because it changes what a good Meta lead looks like. If you judge Meta by how fast leads sign, you will turn it off before it works. Judge it by how many jobs it eventually produces and whether your pipeline is fuller in ninety days.</p>

<h2>What Actually Works Right Now</h2>

<h3>Stop trying to out-target the algorithm</h3>

<p>For years the advice was to stack interests: homeowners, home improvement, specific age brackets, income proxies. That advice is out of date. Meta has pushed its automated audience selection to the point where it is now the default targeting mode for lead campaigns, and for local service work it generally finds buyers faster than hand-built interest stacks once it has conversion data to learn from.</p>

<p>The practical version: give the system your geography, your budget, and good creative, then leave it alone long enough to learn. Constant tinkering resets that learning and is one of the most common reasons a campaign never stabilizes.</p>

<h3>Set geography like a service area, not a metro</h3>

<p>This is where I see the most money wasted. An owner targets the whole metro because that is the default city radius, then pays for leads forty minutes outside the range they will actually drive.</p>

<p>Draw the radius around where your crew realistically works. For most trades that lands somewhere in the fifteen to twenty five mile range, tighter if you are in a dense area, wider if you are rural. If certain suburbs are worth more to you than others, run them as their own campaign rather than averaging them together.</p>

<h3>Exclude the people you already have</h3>

<p>Upload your customer list and exclude it from acquisition campaigns. Exclude recent leads too. Without exclusions you are paying to reach people who already called you, and the reporting will look better than the reality because those conversions were going to happen anyway.</p>

<h2>The Instant Form Trap</h2>

<p>Instant forms are the lead form that opens inside Facebook or Instagram instead of sending someone to your website. They pre-fill name, email, and phone from the person's profile, so submitting takes a couple of taps.</p>

<p>They convert far better than sending cold traffic to a landing page. They are also where most Meta lead quality complaints come from, and the reason is the same: the form is so frictionless that people fill it out half-accidentally while scrolling. You get volume and you get a lot of people who do not remember opting in.</p>

<p>Two things fix most of this.</p>

<p>First, use the higher intent version of the form, which adds a review step before submission. You will get fewer leads and a meaningfully higher share of them will be real.</p>

<p>Second, add one or two qualifying questions. Property type, timeline, or the specific service they want. Every question you add costs you some volume and buys you back quality. For a trade where an estimate means driving out and spending an hour, that is a trade worth making.</p>

<p>One recent change worth knowing: Meta has been rolling out the ability to edit forms directly at the ad level, which previously required duplicating the whole campaign. If you have avoided touching a form because of that hassle, check whether the option is live in your account now.</p>

<h2>Creative Is the Whole Game</h2>

<p>Targeting is largely automated now. Creative is the lever you still fully control, and it is the one that moves cost per lead the most.</p>

<p>For local service businesses the good news is that the best creative is free and already on your phone. Real job photos outperform stock every time. A short before and after clip shot vertically on a jobsite will usually beat anything designed. Video in the Reels format paired with a lead form is currently one of the strongest combinations for local lead generation, and you do not need production quality to use it.</p>

<p>What I look for in a service business ad:</p>

<ul>
<li>A recognizable problem in the first second, not a logo</li>
<li>Actual work, actual crew, actual trucks</li>
<li>A specific location named in the copy so it reads local</li>
<li>One clear action, not three</li>
</ul>

<p>Run several distinct creative concepts at once rather than three versions of the same idea. Different angles teach you which message resonates. Then refresh before performance decays, because the same audience seeing the same ad for two months is the most reliable way to watch your cost per lead climb.</p>

<h2>What Happens After the Lead Comes In</h2>

<p>Meta ads fail more often at the follow-up stage than the ad stage.</p>

<p>A Meta lead was scrolling thirty seconds ago and was not shopping for you. Their interest has a short shelf life. Call in five minutes and it is a conversation. Call the next morning and they have forgotten filling out the form. This is the single biggest difference between owners who say Meta works and owners who say it does not.</p>

<p>That means the leads have to reach you instantly, not sit in a Facebook dashboard nobody logs into. Meta connects directly to most CRM platforms now, so the lead can be in your pipeline and triggering a text within seconds. If you cannot answer the phone during the day because you are on a jobsite, that is exactly the gap <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/ai-automation/">AI lead capture and automation</a> is built to close.</p>

<p>Set up your follow-up before you turn the ads on. Not after.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>

<h3>Should I run Facebook ads or Google ads first?</h3>

<p>If you can only run one, start with Google. It captures people already searching for your service, so it converts faster and proves out sooner. Add Meta once search is working and you want more volume than existing demand provides, or when you are selling something people do not think to search for.</p>

<h3>How long before Facebook ads start working?</h3>

<p>Plan on several weeks before the data means anything. The system needs conversion volume to learn, and early results swing hard in both directions. Judging a campaign after five days is the most common mistake I see. Give it a real window and change one thing at a time.</p>

<h3>Why are my Facebook leads such poor quality?</h3>

<p>Usually the form is too easy to submit and the follow-up is too slow. Switch to the higher intent form option, add a qualifying question or two, and make sure someone or something responds within minutes. Those three changes fix the majority of lead quality complaints I hear.</p>

<h3>Do I need a website if I use instant forms?</h3>

<p>Yes. People who see your ad will look you up before they answer the phone, and a profile with no site behind it costs you credibility at exactly the wrong moment. Your <a href="https://www.evovero.com/services/web-design/">website is the asset you own</a>, while ad platforms are rented attention that stops the day you stop paying.</p>

<h3>Can I run Meta ads myself?</h3>

<p>You can, and plenty of owners do. The setup is not the hard part. The hard part is having enough campaigns running to know what normal looks like, and having the discipline to leave a campaign alone while it learns. That perspective is most of what you pay someone else for.</p>
